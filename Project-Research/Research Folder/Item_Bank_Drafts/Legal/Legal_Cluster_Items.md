## L-APT-1 (original — superseded by revision)

Two classmates disagree about whether a shared online document they both edited can still be used after one of them later claimed the other went beyond what was agreed.

**A)** You look at the original shared understanding, the sequence of edits, and any messages that show what each person actually consented to at the time. ***(correct instinct)*** **[Conscientiousness]**

**B)** You try to get both of them to focus on finishing the work product instead of re-litigating past edits. *(different-priority lean — outcome over process)*

**C)** You suggest they each write down their version of events separately so the group can later decide whose memory is clearer. *(reframe lean — narrative reconstruction)*

**Rationale:** Option A reflects the core qualification en droit public competency of careful analysis of rules, consent, and institutional records rather than personal recollection or immediate practical convenience. It mirrors the structured, evidence-based approach needed for public-law reasoning and institutional roles. B is a legitimate practical priority that privileges completion over formal process. C is a legitimate alternative instinct that treats the conflict as a matter of competing accounts rather than documented agreement.

## L-APT-2

A neighborhood group chat is arguing about whether a recently posted “house rule” for the shared courtyard (quiet hours, parking, etc.) should apply to everyone or only to the people who actively participated in writing it.

**A)** You examine how the rule was announced, whether it was presented as binding on the whole group, and what the usual practice has been for similar notices. ***(correct instinct)*** **[Conscientiousness]**

**B)** You ask whether the people who didn’t participate still benefit from the courtyard and should therefore accept the trade-off. *(different-priority lean — fairness through mutual benefit)*

**C)** You propose testing the rule for two weeks and then revisiting it once people have lived with it. *(wait-and-see / experimental lean)*

**Rationale:** Option A tracks the public-law instinct of examining how a norm was promulgated, its claimed scope, and prior practice — the same analytical habits required for qualification en droit public and work involving institutional or regulatory frameworks. B is a coherent alternative that grounds legitimacy in reciprocal advantage rather than formal process. C is a coherent alternative that privileges provisional experience over immediate formal analysis.

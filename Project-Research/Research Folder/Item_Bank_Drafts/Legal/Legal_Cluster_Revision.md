## L-APT-1 (revised — second pass)

The class representative posts a short notice on the shared class noticeboard (and copies it into the group chat) stating that any slides for the end-of-term presentation not uploaded by Thursday midnight will be left out of the final file. Several students who were offline when the notice appeared say it shouldn’t apply to them because they never saw or agreed to it.

**A)** You check how the notice was worded and where it was posted, whether it was framed as applying to the whole class, and whether previous class notices of this kind were treated as binding once posted. ***(correct instinct)*** **[Conscientiousness]**

**B)** You focus on whether the students who missed the notice still benefit from the shared presentation grade and should therefore accept the same deadline. *(different-priority lean — fairness through mutual benefit)*

**C)** You suggest the group try the Thursday deadline this once and then decide together afterward whether to keep it. *(wait-and-see / experimental lean)*

**Rationale:** Option A reflects the qualification en droit public competency of analyzing how a norm was promulgated (wording + placement), its claimed scope, and prior practice for similar notices — the same institutional-authority questions that arise when determining whether a posted rule binds non-participants. Shifting the notice onto a shared noticeboard (in addition to chat) and referencing “previous class notices of this kind” makes the institutional/promulgation character clearer than pure chat custom. B is a legitimate alternative that grounds legitimacy in reciprocal benefit rather than formal process. C is a legitimate alternative that privileges provisional experience over immediate formal analysis of announcement and precedent.

## L-APT-3 (revised — second pass)

A local amateur football team’s group chat is arguing about whether an old informal practice — letting a player who arrives late still start if they text ahead — still applies after a new captain took over this season.

**A)** You dig into what was actually written or announced in previous seasons, how similar captain changes were handled before, and whether any formal team guidelines still exist that cover the transition. ***(correct instinct)*** **[Conscientiousness]**

**B)** You focus on what would feel fairest to the players who already planned their schedules around the old practice. *(different-priority lean — immediate fairness to current cohort)*

**C)** You propose waiting until the first person actually arrives late and then seeing how the new captain responds in that concrete case. *(wait-and-see lean)*

**Rationale:** Option A reflects the compétences de recherche approfondies competency by treating the question as one that requires looking past the immediate situation to prior practice, written traces, and how similar transitions were handled — rather than deciding from present facts or feelings alone. The amateur-football setting moves the scenario away from a direct school-rule parallel while preserving the same research-into-precedent instinct. B is a legitimate alternative that prioritizes the lived expectations of the current group. C is a legitimate alternative that prefers concrete, case-by-case observation over advance research into precedent.

## L-APT-1 (revised)

In a class WhatsApp group for a shared end-of-term presentation, three students post a message saying any slides not uploaded by Thursday midnight will be left out of the final file. Several classmates who were offline at the time say the rule shouldn’t apply to them because they never agreed to it.

**A)** You check how the message was phrased, whether it was presented as applying to the whole class, and what the group’s usual practice has been when similar deadlines were set before. ***(correct instinct)*** **[Conscientiousness]**

**B)** You focus on whether the students who missed the message still benefit from the shared presentation grade and should therefore accept the same deadline. *(different-priority lean — fairness through mutual benefit)*

**C)** You suggest the group try the Thursday deadline this once and then decide together afterward whether to keep it. *(wait-and-see / experimental lean)*

**Rationale:** Option A reflects the qualification en droit public competency of analyzing how a rule was announced, its claimed scope of application, and prior practice — the same institutional-authority questions that arise when determining whether a norm binds non-participants. B is a legitimate alternative that grounds legitimacy in reciprocal benefit rather than formal process. C is a legitimate alternative that privileges provisional experience over immediate formal analysis of announcement and precedent.

## L-INT-3

A popular student Discord server for sharing notes and exam tips is filling up with low-effort posts that bury the useful threads.

**A)** You look at the existing server rules, how moderation decisions have been made so far, and whether those rules are being applied the same way to everyone. *(Legal/analytical-institutional lean)* **[Conscientiousness]**

**B)** You start thinking of ways to redesign the channels, add pinned guides, or create incentive systems so high-quality posts stay visible and more students keep contributing. *(Business/Enterprising lean)*

## L-APT-3

Your class is arguing about whether the old informal practice of allowing one free makeup presentation still applies after a new coordinator took over the course this semester.

**A)** You dig into what was actually written or announced in previous years, how similar changes of coordinator were handled before, and whether any formal guidelines still exist that cover the transition. ***(correct instinct)*** **[Conscientiousness]**

**B)** You focus on what would feel fairest to the students who already planned their schedules around the old practice. *(different-priority lean — immediate fairness to current cohort)*

**C)** You propose waiting until the first person actually needs a makeup slot and then seeing how the new coordinator responds in that concrete case. *(wait-and-see lean)*

**Rationale:** Option A reflects the compétences de recherche approfondies competency by treating the question as one that requires looking past the immediate situation to prior practice, written traces, and how similar transitions were handled — rather than deciding from present facts or feelings alone. B is a legitimate alternative that prioritizes the lived expectations of the current group. C is a legitimate alternative that prefers concrete, case-by-case observation over advance research into precedent.

## 8. Final Synthesized Recommendation

1. **Format**: Forced-choice situational pairs (not Likert), using O*NET-style concrete scenarios as the item-writing template. **Decision confirmed: going with forced-choice.**
2. **Scoring**: Start simple for v1 — count wins per dimension from forced-choice pairs. Defer full IRT-based multidimensional scoring to v2, once real pilot data exists. Do not let "simple for now" quietly regress into plain Likert.
3. **Delivery**: Adaptive (CAT-style) once a calibrated item bank exists — one statistical investment can likely serve both the adaptive-selection and forced-choice-scoring problems.
4. **Triple layer**: Keep interest (forced-choice, disguised), aptitude (situational, right/wrong, disguised), and personality (forced-choice, descriptive, no right/wrong) as three separate scores. The interest+aptitude combination is untested in the literature — pilot rigorously against real HIS students before trusting its output. Personality carries lower validation risk since there's no "correct answer" at stake.
5. **Bac grades**: Light confidence-modifier only, never a gating score (see Section 6).
6. **Before building anything further**: pilot even a rough version against current HIS students per specialty, and check whether proposed "correct" answers/dimension mappings actually match how strong students in each program really answer. This is what turns the design from "plausible" into "defensible with data."

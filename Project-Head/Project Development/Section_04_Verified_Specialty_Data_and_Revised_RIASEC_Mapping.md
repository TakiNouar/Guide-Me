### 4.2 New finding: admission filière + language split the specialties independently of RIASEC

This wasn't visible before pulling the actual pages, and it matters for both the cluster mapping and the bac-grades layer (Section 6):

- **Only 3 of 8 specialties have a real subject-based prerequisite**: Informatique-SI, Sécurité-SI, and Électronique all require a Sciences expérimentales / Maths / Maths technique bac (Électronique also accepts Génie électrique). These are exactly the technical cluster identified earlier.
- **The other 5 specialties (Droit Public, E-commerce, Économie-Gestion, Psychologie Clinique, and — pending re-verification — Orientation et conseil) are officially open to "toutes filières confondues."** HIS itself doesn't gatekeep these by bac subject.
- **Language of instruction varies independently of both RIASEC and filière**: Droit Public and Psychologie Clinique are taught in **Arabic**; Informatique-SI, Sécurité-SI, Électronique, and Orientation et conseil in **French**; E-commerce and Économie-Gestion in **both**.

**Implication for Section 6 (bac grades):** the subject-to-cluster grade mapping proposed earlier only cleanly applies to the technical cluster, where Math/Physics grades are a real, HIS-endorsed prerequisite signal. For the other 5 specialties, HIS's own admission policy says bac filière/subject grades aren't a gate — so using a specific subject grade as a "modifier" for e.g. Droit Public or Psychologie Clinique would actually contradict HIS's stated open-admission stance for those programs. **A more defensible bac-grade modifier for the non-technical specialties would be Arabic or French language proficiency (matching the specialty's actual language of instruction) rather than a content-subject grade** — since that's the one thing that realistically predicts whether a student can succeed in a program taught in a specific language, without recreating an unofficial subject gate HIS didn't choose to set.

### 4.3 Updated cluster read

The four clusters from the original draft mostly hold, with one internal distinction now backed by real text rather than assumption:

**Cluster 1 — Technical** (Informatique-SI, Sécurité-SI, Électronique): confirmed by shared filière prerequisite. Internal split: Électronique is the most hands-on/hardware (R-dominant); Informatique-SI and Sécurité-SI are both software/systems-oriented (I-C dominant), differing mainly in whether the focus is building systems (Informatique-SI) vs. defending them (Sécurité-SI, with a real pentest/hands-on component).

**Cluster 2 — Business** (E-commerce, Économie-Gestion): confirmed distinct as suspected. E-commerce is digital/creative/customer-facing (touches marketing, design, even light technical dev); Économie-Gestion is quantitative/institutional/analytical. This is now backed by actual verified competency text, not just assumption — a real disambiguation axis exists ("digital-creative" vs. "institutional-analytical") and should be a dedicated question axis in the item bank.

**Cluster 3 — Social/Helping** (Psychologie Clinique, Orientation et conseil): confirmed S-dominant, but Psychologie Clinique's third letter moved from Artistic to Conventional based on verified text — the program is described as methodical/diagnostic/multidisciplinary-team-based, not creative. This matters: it means the disambiguation between Psychologie Clinique and Orientation et conseil should focus on **depth of clinical/pathological focus vs. breadth of institutional guidance**, not on any creative-vs-structured axis.

**Cluster 4 — Droit Public**: revised from C-I-E to **C-E-I** — verified text emphasizes concours prep and advocacy/public-service roles (Enterprising) alongside rigorous research (Conventional), with Investigative now third rather than second. Still sits as a plausible "second choice" bridging Clusters 2 and 3, as originally noted.

**Remaining gap:** "Orientation et conseil" was verified in an earlier pass but not re-checked in this latest sweep alongside the other 7 — worth a final confirmation pass to ensure its admission filière and language data match what's now known about its 7 siblings, for full internal consistency.

### The real problem: specialties cluster into ~4 zones, not 8 distinct profiles

**Cluster 1 — Technical/Investigative:** Informatique-SI, Sécurité-SI, Électronique (all I-dominant, R/C just swap order). Generic RIASEC items won't tell these apart — "I like solving problems" is true for all three. Needs HIS-specific disambiguation items contrasting e.g. security-mindset vs. build-an-app-end-to-end vs. physical-circuit-and-sensor work.

**Cluster 2 — Business/Enterprising:** E-commerce, Économie et Gestion (both E-C dominant). Real differentiator may not be RIASEC at all — more like "digital/customer-facing vs. structural/internal management." May need a dimension outside pure RIASEC.

**Cluster 3 — Social/Helping:** Psychologie Clinique, Orientation et conseil (both S-dominant). Differentiator: clinical/therapeutic depth (understanding pathology) vs. institutional guidance/mentorship (helping someone choose a next step). Needs items that separate these specifically.

**Cluster 4 — Droit Public:** Sits semi-alone (C-I-E) with overlap risk against both Cluster 2 (via E) and Cluster 3 (via advocacy/E) — actually useful as a natural "second choice" for students torn between technical rigor and people-facing work.

**Implication for test design:** The adaptive test's broad-scan phase only needs to identify *which cluster* a student lands in (cheap, generic RIASEC territory). The real question budget should go toward cluster-specific disambiguation items written using HIS's actual program language, once a student is flagged as ambiguous within a cluster.

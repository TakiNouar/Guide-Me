### 10.1 International University Orientation Test Practice

> ⚠️ **Unverified sources in this table:** Harvard, Stanford, KAUST, UCL, Sorbonne/Sciences Po, METU, and the Tsinghua SCL-90 policy claim are sourced from the research assistants but have **not** been independently re-checked with a live link. Oxford, Koç, Qatar University, Helsinki, and Tokyo **have** been independently verified (see the standalone Wider Research Findings report for links). Flagged here rather than fixed — re-verification isn't a current priority.

General pattern across all 9 countries researched (US, UK, France, Turkey, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Finland, Japan, China): **top universities rarely mandate a proprietary orientation/career test at enrollment.** Career-specific testing is almost always opt-in, delivered later by a Career Services department — not part of mandatory orientation. Where mandatory testing *does* appear at orientation, it's typically **wellbeing/mental-health screening**, not career-fit testing (see Helsinki, Tsinghua below) — a distinct category from what we're building.

| University | Test | What it measures | Purpose | How it's used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Harvard (US) | Strong Interest Inventory & MBTI (optional) | Career interests, personality preferences | Career exploration | Via Office of Career Services, on request; not part of mandatory orientation |
| Stanford (US) | CliftonStrengths (optional) | Top 5 natural talents | Leadership/team building | Used in leadership development programs, not orientation sorting |
| Oxford (UK) | **Career Weaver** (in-house tool; corrected from an earlier unverified claim) | Values, work style, strengths, motivations — deliberately does not generate "matches" | Building self-awareness as first step of career planning | Accessed directly via SSO; MBTI/DISC available as optional paid add-ons via advisor referral |
| UCL (UK) | No mandatory test; SHL/Myers-Briggs offered optionally | Personality, occupational fit | Interview/assessment-center prep | Resource for upper-year students job-hunting |
| Sorbonne / Sciences Po (France) | No specific test | — | — | Orientation managed via Parcoursup nationally; SCUIO offers 1-on-1 qualitative counseling instead of standardized testing |
| METU (Turkey) | No specific test | — | — | Placement via national YKS exam; orientation is informational only |
| Koç University (Turkey) | No verifiable named test (corrected from an earlier unverified claim) | — | Advisor-guided self-assessment | Delivered via mandatory advisor meetings + "E.D.I.T. Your Career" framework and UNIV 101 seminar, not a standalone public test |
| KAUST (Saudi Arabia) | CliftonStrengths | Personal strengths | Professional development | Used to build cohesive lab environments/personal development plans |
| Qatar University | **Tamheed (تمهيد)** (corrected from an earlier unverified claim) | Abilities, inclinations, personal qualities, talents | Helps undecided/transfer-considering students explore aligned disciplines | ~30-min online assessment (Arabic/English) via dedicated portal; results reviewed in a required advisor session |
| Helsinki (Finland) | HowULearn | Student wellbeing, learning approaches, study load | Academic support (not career) | Mandatory survey at orientation and throughout the degree, monitors cohort wellbeing |
| U. Tokyo (Japan) | SPI (Synthetic Personality Inventory) | Aptitude + personality | Pre-employment screening | **Administered by employers, not the university** — UTokyo's Career Support Room only runs prep workshops for it (self-analysis, interview/group-discussion practice) |
| Tsinghua (China) | SCL-90 | Psychological symptoms, mental health baseline | Wellbeing screening | Administered to incoming freshmen at orientation to flag students needing mental-health support; career testing kept separate/optional |

**Takeaway for our project:** no direct precedent exists for a mandatory, university-run, specialty-matching test at orientation the way we're designing — most institutions either skip career testing entirely at this stage or keep it opt-in and advisor-mediated. This actually strengthens the case for HIS's approach being a genuine differentiator, not a gap we're failing to fill compared to peers. The Tamheed (Qatar University) model is the closest real precedent — worth a closer look as a comparison case since it's specifically built for undecided/transfer students choosing a major, delivered digitally, followed by mandatory advisor review — structurally similar to our own pipeline (test → confidence label → advisor conversation).

### 10.4 Psychological/Emotional-State Screening Scales

Distinct from our own motivation/readiness signal (Section 7.3) — these are general wellbeing screeners, not career-decision tools:

| Scale | Measures | Purpose | Target population / use in orientation |
|---|---|---|---|
| DASS-21 | Depression, anxiety, stress (three separate subscales) | Isolate which negative state is most prominent | Students showing signs of distress at intake; determines academic counseling vs. clinical referral |
| GAD-7 | Severity of generalized anxiety | Quick anxiety screening | Students paralyzed by decision-making/exhibiting panic signs; flags need for medical/psychological referral |
| PHQ-9 | Severity of depressive symptoms | Screening, diagnosis, monitoring of depression | Students showing apathy, withdrawal, or a sudden performance drop |
| PSS-10 | Perceived stress from life situations | Measures unpredictability/overload as experienced | General student body during transitions (orientation, midterms) — non-clinical, flags need for time-management support |

**Relevance to our project:** these belong to the Orientation Department's broader toolkit, not to our specialty-matching test itself — but worth flagging as a natural trigger point: if our test's "high anxiety" signals (e.g., inconsistent answers, very low confidence across all clusters) correlate with a student who might benefit from one of these screeners, that's a reasonable escalation path for advisors, not something our engine should attempt to diagnose itself.

### 10.5 Preliminary Assessment Scale Model (Task 2)

The preliminary model/concept for the HIS specialty assessment scale, as requested — dimensions plus example items per dimension, drawn from the design work already completed earlier in this project (Sections 2-5).

**Format:** Forced-choice, situational scenarios (not Likert-rated) — see Section 2.1 for rationale.

**Four dimensions:**

| Dimension | What it measures | Right/wrong answer? | Theoretical source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interest | Which of HIS's 8 specialties/4 clusters a student is drawn to | No — relative preference only | Holland's RIASEC (Section 7.1) |
| Aptitude | Whether a student's real-life instincts match what a field actually demands | Yes — scored against a "correct instinct" per item | Custom-built (Section 2.3); no direct literature precedent |
| Academic fit | Light, non-gating confidence modifier | No — descriptive/adjusting only | Bac grades — subject-based for technical cluster, language-based for the rest (Section 6) |
| Personality | Working-style fit (Big Five) | No — descriptive only, no correct profile | IPIP (Section 7.3) |

**Example item per dimension:**

*Interest:*
> A friend's laptop breaks the night before a big deadline.
> A) You try to find out why — you check one thing at a time until you find the problem.
> B) You stay with your friend, help them feel calm, and find a solution together.

*Aptitude (disguised, technical cluster):*
> Movie night. The projector won't connect. You already tried a new cable.
> A) You check the settings, one by one, until you find the problem. *(correct instinct)*
> B) You ask someone who "knows computers" to fix it.
> C) You give up and just watch on a laptop.

*Academic fit:* not a question item — pulled directly from the student's bac transcript (Math+Physics for technical specialties; Arabic/French proficiency for the rest).

*Personality:*
> You have a free afternoon to work on a school project.
> A) You'd rather sit alone and focus deeply until it's done. *(Conscientiousness / low Extraversion)*
> B) You'd rather work with others, bouncing ideas around as you go. *(Extraversion)*

**Scoring at a glance:** each of the three question-based dimensions is tallied independently (win-rate for Interest, correct-rate for Aptitude, trait-rate for Personality — full formulas in Section 5), then combined into a labeled recommendation, never a single blended score (Section 5, Step 5).

**Status:** this is a preliminary model, not a finished item bank — the full ~30-item v1 bank (Section 9, item #3) is still open work. This section exists to give the team a concrete shape to react to before that larger build starts.
